Replace or Repair Garage Door Springs in Fishers

By Goat Garage Door Service Editorial Team  ·  3 min read

You should repair garage door springs only when the springs are relatively new and the problem is limited to adjustment, minor corrosion, or a single component such as a winding cone; replace springs when they are worn, past expected cycles, or when one spring of a matched pair has failed. Safety and balance determine the right choice, so a professional inspection is the fastest way to a safe outcome.

Start here: if your door behaves oddly, do not use the opener and keep people away from the door. Read the sections below to weigh repair against replacement, understand the costs and risks, and prepare for a technician visit.

TL;DR

  • Repair can be cost-effective for recent, minor spring problems, but only if the spring still has significant life left.
  • If one spring in a matched pair fails, replacing both springs is usually safer and more reliable.
  • Spring work is hazardous; have a trained technician evaluate balance, cables, and related parts before using the door.
  • Ask a technician about spring cycle ratings and how replacement springs will match your door weight and opener.

How springs fail and what that means for repair

Torsion and extension springs both have finite lifespans measured in cycles, where one cycle equals one full open and close. Wear appears as rusted or broken coils, frayed windings, uneven door motion, and extra stress on the opener.

  • If the spring shows only surface rust or loose fasteners, a technician may be able to adjust or lubricate components and replace small parts.
  • If windings are separated, there are gaps, or the spring has already snapped, replacement is necessary.

Repair is reasonable when the underlying metal is sound and the remaining cycle life is high. Replacement is the safer choice when metal fatigue is evident or the springs are original to an older installation.

Costs and related work to expect

Several factors affect cost and scope:

  • Spring type: torsion springs generally cost more than extension springs but perform differently.
  • Number of springs: two-spring systems will usually require both springs replaced if one fails.
  • Door weight and size: heavier doors need springs rated for higher torque.
  • Associated parts: cables, drums, rollers, hinges, and brackets are often inspected and sometimes replaced when springs are replaced to ensure safe operation.

Preparing for the service call

Prepare your garage and yourself so the appointment goes smoothly:

  • Clear floor space around the door so the technician can access the tracks and jambs.
  • If possible, note when the problem started and any noises or events that preceded the failure.
  • Keep pets and children away during the inspection and any repair work.
  • ✓Have the door remote or keypad ready if the technician asks to verify opener operation.
  • ✓Note any recent repairs or parts replaced, for example an opener installation, that might affect diagnosis.

When to call for emergency garage door repair

Certain situations require prompt professional attention:

  • The spring has snapped and the door will not stay up.
  • The door suddenly begins closing too quickly or unevenly.
  • You hear a loud breaking sound and see visible damage to springs or cables.

In those cases, avoid trying to open or prop the door yourself. Contact a trained repair service to secure and repair the door. Goat Garage Door Service lists its services and how they handle common repairs on the home page.

Warning signs a spring needs professional attention

If you see any of the following, do not try to operate the door and call a trained technician.

  • Door will not open or closes unevenly
    Stop using the opener and schedule inspection
  • Loud bang or abrupt break sound
    Keep people and pets away from the door and secure the area
  • Visible gaps, rust, or frayed windings on the spring
    Avoid touching the spring and request a replacement estimate
  • Opener motor strains or runs without moving the door
    Do not continue use, the opener and springs must be evaluated together

Expert note

Expert note: Replacing only the visibly broken spring can save money now but may leave you at risk of another imminent failure if springs are matched. A matched pair balanced for your door weight reduces strain on your opener and prolongs the life of all components.

Frequently asked questions

How long should my garage door spring last?

Most springs have a rated life measured in cycles. A common standard is 10,000 cycles for many residential springs, which translates to several years depending on how often you open the door. Environmental factors and maintenance also affect life. Goat Garage Door Service recommends a professional inspection when springs approach their rated cycles so you can plan repair or replacement before failure.

Can I use my garage door if one spring is broken?

You should avoid using the door if a spring is broken. A single broken spring can leave the door unbalanced and put excessive strain on the opener and cables, creating safety hazards. Keep people and pets clear and have a technician assess and secure the door as soon as possible.

How long does it take to change a spring on a garage door?

The actual replacement of a spring typically takes a trained technician between 30 minutes and 90 minutes, depending on whether other components need attention. The full appointment may take longer to include inspection, balance testing, and any related part replacements. A service provider will give a time estimate after their initial inspection.

Can you replace garage door springs?

Yes, a trained garage door technician can replace springs safely and match new springs to your door's weight and specifications. Spring replacement requires the proper tools and expertise because springs are under high tension. Contact a professional service to schedule an inspection and replacement.
